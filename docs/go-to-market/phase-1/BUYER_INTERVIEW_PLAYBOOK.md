# Buyer Interview Playbook

**Goal:** Complete five useful 15-minute conversations before changing the offer or niche.
**Recommended mix:** Three industrial marketing/sales leaders and two relevant agency owners or producers.

---

## 1. What this interview is—and is not

This is market research, not a disguised sales call.

We are trying to learn:

- How product launches and trade-show campaigns really work.
- Where enquiries, RFQs and follow-up break down.
- Which problem is expensive enough to pay to solve.
- What makes an external vendor feel risky.
- Which words buyers themselves use to describe the problem.

Do not show a full deck, diagnose the entire business for free or force a pitch. If the person describes a real problem and asks about help, ask permission to schedule a separate commercial call.

## 2. Who to invite first

Priority order:

1. Existing or second-degree contacts who work in industrial marketing, sales or operations.
2. Leaders at medium/high-confidence target accounts.
3. Industrial agency owners, executive producers or strategy directors.
4. Manufacturers outside the top-30 list who still match the ICP.

Avoid interviewing only friends, junior employees or people who cannot describe the buying and approval process.

## 3. LinkedIn invitation — under 300 characters

> Hi [First Name]—I’m researching how industrial teams handle product launches and trade-show enquiries. I’m speaking with a few marketing/sales leaders for 15 minutes; this is research, not a sales pitch. Your perspective at [Company] would be genuinely useful.

## 4. Message after connection acceptance

> Thanks, [First Name]. I’m studying where complex-product launches lose momentum between content, buyer enquiry and sales follow-up. Could I ask you 7–8 practical questions in a 15-minute call? I won’t pitch during the interview. I can also send you a short anonymized summary of what I learn.

## 6. Follow-ups

### Follow-up 1 — after 3 business days

> Hi [First Name]—bringing this back once in case it was buried. I’m specifically trying to understand [one relevant issue, e.g. how IMTS leads move from booth scan to a complete RFQ]. A 15-minute practitioner view would be very helpful. No pitch during the call.

### Follow-up 2 — after another 4–5 business days

> Last note from me, [First Name]. If you’re not the right person, is there someone who owns product-launch content or trade-show lead follow-up at [Company]? Either way, thank you.

Stop after this. Persistence is professional; repeated unwanted contact is not.

## 7. 15-minute call structure

### Minute 0–1: Set expectations

> Thank you for the time. I’m researching how industrial teams move from product attention to a useful sales conversation. I have eight practical questions and I’ll keep this to 15 minutes. I’m not going to pitch you. Is it okay if I take written notes? I won’t record without your explicit permission.

### Minutes 1–12: Ask and follow the evidence

Ask these core questions:

1. Walk me through how a new product launch is currently planned—from first concept to sales follow-up.
2. When someone submits an enquiry, what must happen before sales considers it a useful or qualified lead?
3. Roughly how long does enquiry-to-first-response and enquiry-to-quote take today?
4. After a trade show, how are badge scans, notes and follow-ups managed? Where do leads usually get stuck?
5. Which repetitive manual task creates the most delay or frustration?
6. What is the hardest part of explaining or showing a complex product through video/content?
7. If you could improve one metric this year, which would have the most business value—and why?
8. Who normally approves a project that combines campaign content with a sales/CRM workflow?
9. When hiring an external creative or software partner, what feels most risky?

You will not always ask all nine. Follow useful answers and protect the 15-minute promise.

### Strong follow-up prompts

- “Can you give me a recent example?”
- “What happens next?”
- “How is that measured today?”
- “How often does that occur?”
- “What does that delay cost—in time, lost enquiries or rework?”
- “Who feels that pain most?”
- “What have you already tried?”

Avoid leading questions such as “Wouldn’t an AI chatbot solve that?” They produce agreement, not truth.

### Minutes 12–15: Close cleanly

> That was genuinely helpful. May I send one short follow-up if I need to confirm a detail? I’ll also share the anonymized pattern summary when the five interviews are complete.

If a real commercial problem appears:

> You mentioned [problem] is causing [impact]. I have a few ideas, but I promised not to pitch during this interview. Would it be useful to schedule a separate 20-minute working session to explore it?

## 9. Language-mining rules

After five interviews, make a simple frequency table:

- Exact phrase used.
- Number of interviews mentioning it.
- Role(s) mentioning it.
- Operational or financial impact.
- Current workaround.
- Whether the buyer actively wants it solved.

Use repeated buyer language in the website and outreach. Do not turn a single interesting comment into a market-wide claim.

## 10. Interview success criteria

An interview counts only when:

- The person matches the market or partner channel.
- The conversation actually happened.
- At least one current-process fact and one commercial-risk insight were captured.
- Exact language was recorded accurately.
- The next action is documented.

A booked call, LinkedIn reply or friendly compliment is not a completed interview.
